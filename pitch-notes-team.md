# Presenting Sunny — team cheat sheet

You do not need to understand the code. You need to own **one idea**, and it's an idea you
can explain to anyone.

---

## The one idea

> **The maths decides who gets flagged. The AI only writes the sentence.**

That's it. Everything else on slides 6 and 7 is that sentence with detail attached. If you
remember nothing else, you can hold the whole middle of this pitch on that line.

Why it matters: schools are nervous about AI making judgements about children — and they're
right to be. Ours doesn't. A machine that does arithmetic and shows its working is
something a principal can defend to parents. That's our whole competitive advantage.

---

## Slide 6 — "How a signal is produced"

Four boxes, left to right. Three say **No AI**. One says **AI**.

**What to say:**

> "Detection is arithmetic. The AI only writes the wording. We never mix the two.
>
> Step one is the data — this child's last thirty school days.
> Step two is the detection — four fixed rules. No AI at all.
> Step three is the only place AI appears, and all it does is turn a fired rule into a
> sentence a teacher can read.
> Step four, the teacher decides. Always."

Then point at the gold bar and slow down:

> "Remove the AI entirely, and Sunny flags exactly the same children."

**That last line is the most important sentence in the pitch.** Say it slowly. Pause after.

**What NOT to say:** don't say the AI "analyses" anything, or "learns", or "understands".
It doesn't. It writes one sentence from a rule name and five numbers.

---

## Slide 7 — "Four rules, printed in full"

Don't read all four aloud. Gesture at them and make the point about *why they fit on a slide*.

**What to say:**

> "Four rules. That's all of them — there is no fifth one hidden in a model somewhere.
> A parent could check this maths by hand.
>
> [point left box] Every rule compares a child only to themselves. A child who checks in
> 'okay' most days is never flagged for it. That's their normal, and normal is not a
> problem. That matters enormously for quiet and neurodivergent kids — it stops them being
> flagged for simply being who they are.
>
> [point right box] And this one we're proud of. A mood scale can't say 'I have stopped
> answering'. A child who quietly disengages produces no low scores at all — so without
> that fourth rule, they'd be invisible at exactly the moment you most want to see them."

**If you only get one extra sentence in Q&A, use the "compared only to themselves" one.**

---

## The five things worth knowing

1. **Children tap a face, not words.** No password, no vocabulary, no metaphor to decode.
   Twenty seconds.
2. **Every child is compared only to their own normal**, never to classmates.
3. **Four rules do the detecting. AI only phrases the suggestion for the teacher.**
4. **A parent or teacher can write a support plan** — what helps this child — and it appears
   *above* the AI's suggestion. The people who know the child outrank the machine.
5. **Nothing happens automatically.** Every signal ends with a human deciding. Sunny never
   contacts anyone.

## Three questions you can answer yourself

**"How do you know the child is sad?"**
> "We don't — and the app says so on screen, every time. All we detect is that a number the
> child chose has moved away from their own normal. It could be a cold, a bad night's sleep,
> a hard test. The screen lists those possibilities itself."

**"Isn't this surveillance of children?"**
> "It's one tap a day, and the child can see everything we hold about them. No free text, no
> camera, no microphone. It's deleted after thirty days and never trains a model. And a child
> can always choose 'I'd rather not say'."

**"What if the teacher disagrees with a signal?"**
> "They dismiss it, and it clears. We keep a record of dismissals so we can publish an honest
> false-positive rate rather than pretend we don't have one."

## When to hand over

If a judge asks about medians, thresholds, code, the server, or "is the AI real" —
**hand it to Arthur.** Say it plainly:

> "That's Arthur's area — Arthur?"

This is not a weakness. In a team pitch it reads as a group that knows its roles. What
reads badly is guessing and being wrong.

---

## Two things never to say

**Don't say the AI decides anything.** It doesn't. If you say it does, you've handed a judge
the exact criticism our whole design exists to avoid.

**Don't say the data is real.** It's entirely synthetic — we invented the class. No real
child was involved at any point. If asked, say that immediately and without hesitation; it's
a strength, not an admission.

---

## On the day

- Speak slowly. Everyone speeds up under pressure; you will too.
- The live demo is the moment judges remember. When visitors' faces appear on the board,
  **stop talking and let them look.**
- We are stopped at 10 minutes. Rehearse with a timer at least twice tonight.
